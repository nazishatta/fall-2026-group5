\section{Overview}

Phase 3 asks whether the frozen Fashion-MNIST network and its 15$\times$15 SOM can tell when an image is \emph{unlike} the training data (a ``novel'' or out-of-distribution image). The design is the MNIST Phase 3 design, copied unchanged and written down before any Fashion-MNIST novelty score was computed. There are two tests:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Far novelty:} the 26,032 SVHN test images (house-number photographs \cite{netzer2011reading}) against the 10,500 Fashion-MNIST validation images. These are the same SVHN images used for MNIST.
  \item \textbf{Near novelty:} clothing classes the network has never seen. In 5 class splits a new network and map are trained on 6 classes and tested on the other 4 \cite{neal2018open}.
\end{itemize}
The Fashion-MNIST test split is not used in Phase 3.

\textbf{Summary of the results.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{The SOM detects SVHN, but weakly.} Its quantization error (QE) gives AUROC 0.789, well above chance (0.500), but far below the network's energy score (0.982) and max-softmax (0.884). On MNIST, QE gave 0.981 on the same SVHN images.
  \item \textbf{The fixed QE alarm does not work on SVHN.} At the threshold set from the training images it flags only 3.0\% of SVHN, fewer than the 5.1\% of normal validation images it flags. On MNIST it caught 96.2\%.
  \item \textbf{SVHN is mostly told apart by one simple feature.} Its embeddings are much shorter than Fashion-MNIST's; length alone separates the two sets almost perfectly (AUROC 0.995). QE follows length only weakly, which explains its weak result.
  \item \textbf{On unseen clothing classes the SOM beats max-softmax.} QE is higher than max-softmax in all 5 splits (+0.046 AUROC, 95\% interval +0.034 to +0.057). On MNIST this comparison was inconclusive.
  \item \textbf{Plain nearest-neighbour distance beats the SOM in both tests.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Key terms in plain words}

{\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.12}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.26\textwidth}|X|}
\hline
\textbf{Term} & \textbf{What it means} \\ \hline
Normal / novel images & Normal images come from the same source as the training data (Fashion-MNIST validation). Novel images do not (SVHN, or clothing classes the network never saw). \\ \hline
Far / near novelty & Far: novel images that look very different (street-number photos). Near: novel images that look similar (another kind of clothing). \\ \hline
Quantization error (QE) & The distance from an image's embedding to its best SOM cell. A large QE means the image does not fit the map well. \\ \hline
U-matrix score & How far the image's cell is from its neighbouring cells; high on the borders between groups. \\ \hline
Max-softmax, energy & Two scores from the network's own output. Low confidence (max-softmax) or high energy suggests a novel image \cite{hendrycks2017baseline,liu2020energy}. \\ \hline
Mahalanobis, nearest neighbour & Two distances in the embedding space: to the class centres \cite{lee2018mahalanobis}, and to the 5 closest training embeddings \cite{sun2022knn}. \\ \hline
AUROC & How well a score ranks novel images above normal ones: 1 is perfect, 0.5 is chance, below 0.5 means the score is the wrong way round. \\ \hline
AUPR-out & Precision–recall area with novel images as positives. Its chance level here is 0.713, because 71\% of the images are SVHN. \\ \hline
FPR at 95\% TPR & The share of normal images wrongly flagged when 95\% of the novel images are caught. Lower is better. \\ \hline
Paired interval & A 95\% range for the difference between two scores, computed on the same resampled images. \\ \hline
\end{tabularx}}

\section{Related Work}

Kohonen's SOM \cite{kohonen1990som} gives a set of prototypes on a grid, and the distance to the nearest prototype (QE) is a natural novelty signal; we use the NNSOM implementation \cite{jafari_nnsom} and do not change the map. Hendrycks and Gimpel \cite{hendrycks2017baseline} showed that the network's top softmax probability is a strong, simple novelty baseline, and Liu et al.\ \cite{liu2020energy} replaced it with an energy score computed from all the logits. Lee et al.\ \cite{lee2018mahalanobis} measure the distance to class centres in feature space, and Sun et al.\ \cite{sun2022knn} the distance to the nearest training features; we compute both on the same \texttt{fc2} embeddings as the SOM, so the comparison isolates what the map adds. Isolation Forest \cite{liu2008isolation}, the One-Class SVM \cite{scholkopf2001support} and autoencoder reconstruction error \cite{sakurada2014autoencoder} are the classical detectors named in the project proposal. Nalisnick et al.\ \cite{nalisnick2019deep} showed that a model of normal data can rate SVHN as \emph{more} typical than its own data; we see the same reversal for two classical detectors. For near novelty we follow the open-set protocol of Neal et al.\ \cite{neal2018open}, where max-softmax is a hard baseline to beat \cite{vaze2022openset}.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Frozen inputs.} The Phase 1 network (\texttt{lenet5\_fashion\_mnist\_s42}) and the Phase 2 headline SOM (\texttt{som\_15x15\_nb11\_ep250\_s42}), each checked by its SHA-256 before every run.
\item \textbf{Outside set.} All 26,032 SVHN test images, in file order, converted exactly as for MNIST: RGB to grey, resized from $32\times32$ to $28\times28$, pixels scaled to $[0,1]$. Their digit labels are not used.
\item \textbf{Scores.} S1 QE, S2 U-matrix, S3 max-softmax, S4 energy, S5 Mahalanobis (shared Ledoit--Wolf covariance \cite{ledoit2004wellconditioned}), S6 mean distance to the 5 nearest training embeddings. Anything fitted is fitted on the training embeddings only.
\item \textbf{Measures.} AUROC with a bootstrap 95\% interval (2,000 resamples, seed 42) \cite{fawcett2006roc}, AUPR-out \cite{saito2015pr}, FPR at 95\% TPR, and paired bootstrap intervals for QE minus each other score. Control: the QE AUROC with the labels shuffled 1,000 times.
\item \textbf{Statements, fixed before the run.} O1: the SOM detects SVHN (QE interval above 0.5). \textbf{O2 (primary):} the SOM is competitive with max-softmax (lower end of the QE minus max-softmax interval above $-0.01$). O3: the same against energy, Mahalanobis and nearest neighbour, one by one. O4: the U-matrix detects SVHN. Each is met, not met, or inconclusive.
\item \textbf{QE alarm.} An image is flagged if its QE is above the 95th percentile of the training QE (1.4215). Other percentiles are reported only as a sweep; none is chosen from it.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{The SOM sees SVHN as unusual, but not strongly.} With shuffled labels the QE AUROC averages 0.500 (largest of 1,000 shuffles: 0.510), so 0.789 is far from chance (O1 met). The U-matrix score does a little better (0.812, O4 met).
\item \textbf{The network's own scores are clearly better} (O2 not met). Energy almost separates the two sets perfectly (0.982); max-softmax and nearest-neighbour distance are also ahead of QE. Only Mahalanobis is behind.

\end{itemize}

\section{The Fixed QE Alarm}\label{sec:qeflag}

At the 95th percentile of the training QE (1.4215) the alarm flags 536 of the 10,500 validation images (5.1\%) but only 792 of the 26,032 SVHN images (3.0\%). It flags \emph{fewer} novel images than normal ones, so as a yes/no detector it does not work here. Table~\ref{tab:sweep} shows that this holds for every stricter cut-off as well; only the looser 90th percentile flags slightly more SVHN than validation images. The SOM still ranks most SVHN images above most validation images (AUROC 0.789), but SVHN sits in the middle of the QE range, below the upper tail of the normal images, where any threshold must be placed.

\section{U-Matrix and Where SVHN Lands}\label{sec:umatrix}

On the validation images, QE and the U-matrix score have a Spearman correlation of 0.38: they are related but measure different things. Figure~\ref{fig:overlay} shows where the SVHN images land on the map. All 26,032 fall in only 42 of the 225 cells, and two cells, 109 and 94, receive 11,977 and 11,298 images (89\% together). Both lie in the region whose most common Fashion-MNIST class is \emph{Bag}. In total 91\% of SVHN lands in Bag cells, and these hold 92\% of the missed images. A further 9\% lands in mixed Pullover and Dress cells. A missed SVHN image has a QE only slightly above that of the normal images in the same cell (median ratio 1.04). So, to the map, SVHN looks like a typical bag. This is a description of where the misses are, not a proof of why.

\section{Why SVHN Is Missed: Embedding Length}\label{sec:length}

These checks were run because the same problem appeared as on MNIST, and are exploratory.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{SVHN embeddings are short.} The average length of an SVHN embedding is 8.5, against 20.7 for Fashion-MNIST; 95\% of SVHN images are below 11.7, and 95\% of validation images are above 13.2. A rule that scores ``shorter means more novel'' separates the two sets almost perfectly (AUROC 0.995).
\item \textbf{Not a brightness effect.} SVHN images are often dark digits on a bright background, the opposite of Fashion-MNIST. Inverting their grey levels lengthens the embeddings only from 8.50 to 9.18 on average.
\item \textbf{QE follows length only weakly.} With the validation and SVHN images pooled, QE and embedding length have a Spearman correlation of $-0.29$ (MNIST: $-0.83$); within the validation images it is $-0.02$ and within SVHN $+0.13$. Keeping only images whose length lies in the 5--95\% range of the training lengths (13.2 to 31.8) leaves 9,478 validation images but only 532 SVHN images. On that subset QE's AUROC is 0.958 (0.953--0.962), against 0.789 on all images. This fits the idea that the short embeddings are what hide SVHN from QE, but 532 selected images are too few to show it.
\end{itemize}
So on Fashion-MNIST, SVHN differs from the normal images mainly in one simple feature, which a trivial rule already uses. A far-novelty AUROC on SVHN largely measures how strongly a score follows that feature. On MNIST the length effect helped QE; on Fashion-MNIST it works against it.

\section{Leakage Control}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The Phase 1 network and the Phase 2 map are frozen and checked by SHA-256. The SOM scaler, the Mahalanobis means and covariance, the nearest-neighbour reference, the classical detectors and the QE threshold are all fitted on the Fashion-MNIST training embeddings.
  \item The SVHN images are used only to compute scores; nothing is fitted or chosen on them, and their digit labels are not used.
  \item The scores, measures, statements and verdict rules were written in the Fashion-MNIST protocol (with amendments A1 and A2) before any Fashion-MNIST novelty score existed. The length and autoencoder diagnostics were run because the protocol says to run them if the MNIST problems reappear; they are exploratory.
  \item The held-out-class networks never see their unknown classes, and only training and validation images are used.
  \item The Fashion-MNIST test split is not read in Phase 3. Every run records the SHA-256 of its inputs, and no finished run is overwritten.
  \item \textbf{Declared:} the protocol was written after the MNIST results were known. It copies MNIST's design rather than adapting it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item One network and one map for the SVHN test, so the intervals show only which images were sampled. The SOM seed alone moves the QE AUROC by about 0.1.
  \item Only one outside set. SVHN is far novelty and differs mainly in embedding length, so it says little about images that look like clothing; near novelty is covered only by unseen Fashion-MNIST classes.
  \item The held-out-class splits share classes (Sandal is unseen in all five), so they are not independent.
  \item The comparisons with nine methods and the length checks are exploratory.
  \item The figures are kept in DVC; the ones shown here were copied from their run folders.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion and Next Phase}

On Fashion-MNIST the SOM detects both kinds of novelty better than chance, but its strengths differ from MNIST. Against far novelty (SVHN) it is clearly behind the network's own energy and confidence scores, and its fixed alarm does not work, because SVHN's short embeddings make it look like a typical bag to the map. Against near novelty (unseen clothing classes) it beats max-softmax in every split, which it did not do on MNIST. Plain nearest-neighbour distance on the same embeddings beats the SOM in both tests, so the map's added value is again the picture it gives of \emph{where} novel images land. Phase 4 uses the map's hotspots to guide retraining and repeats these novelty tests on the retrained networks.
