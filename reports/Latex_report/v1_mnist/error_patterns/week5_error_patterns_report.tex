\section{Week 5 Overview}
Week 5 analyzes classifier error geography on the frozen 15$\times$15 SOM. The analysis uses the frozen validation embeddings and the BMU assignments produced during Week 4. No CNN retraining or SOM retraining was performed. The held-out test split was intentionally excluded from hotspot discovery.

The goal is to determine whether validation mistakes are distributed uniformly across the representation space or whether they concentrate in specific SOM neurons. The analysis combines local error rate, raw error count, support-aware Wilson confidence bounds, hotspot detection, confusion-pair summaries, and representative MNIST images recovered from the original data.

\section{Validation Error Summary}
The validation split contains 10,500 samples. The baseline produces 10,408 correct predictions and 92 misclassifications, corresponding to a global validation error rate of approximately \textbf{0.876\%}. Error hotspots are defined using a support-aware development rule: a neuron must contain at least 20 validation samples and the 95\% Wilson lower bound of its local error rate must exceed the global validation error rate.

\section{Confusion Geography}
The most frequent validation confusion pairs were 5$\rightarrow$0, 9$\rightarrow$4, and 4$\rightarrow$9, each with six errors overall, followed by 9$\rightarrow$8 and 5$\rightarrow$3 with five errors each. Hotspot localization adds an important spatial dimension: all four observed 9$\rightarrow$7 errors occur inside detected hotspot neurons, while three of six 4$\rightarrow$9 errors and three of five 5$\rightarrow$3 errors occur inside hotspots. This indicates that some confusion types are substantially more concentrated in SOM space than others.

\section{Interpretation and Leakage Control}
The results show that validation errors are not uniformly distributed across the SOM. Under the predefined development rule, 9 of 225 neurons contain 27 of the 92 validation errors. This concentration supports the capstone hypothesis that SOM topology can expose structured failure geography that is not visible in a single global accuracy value.

The hotspot criterion remains descriptive and development-stage. It does not establish statistical significance, causality, out-of-distribution detection performance, or improvement from SOM-guided retraining. The hotspot rule was developed and applied using validation data only. The held-out test split remains untouched and should only be used after the criterion and downstream evaluation procedure are frozen.

\section{Conclusion}
Week 5 localized classifier failures within the representation geometry established by the frozen SOM. Although the global validation error rate is only 0.876\%, a small subset of nine neurons captures 29.35\% of all validation mistakes. Neuron 67 is the strongest concentration, with six errors among 35 samples and a local error rate of 17.14\%. The representative-image panels further show that these hotspots contain difficult or ambiguous handwriting patterns, connecting neuron-level statistics back to concrete input examples.

This analysis moves the project beyond aggregate accuracy toward structured diagnosis of where and how the classifier fails. The next phase can use these frozen development findings to study novelty scoring, boundary behavior, or carefully controlled retraining strategies without using the held-out test split for model-development decisions.
