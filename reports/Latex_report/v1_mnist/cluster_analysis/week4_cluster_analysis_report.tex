\section{Week 4 Overview}
Week 4 extends the trained MNIST Self-Organizing Map from visualization to quantitative cluster analysis. The analysis uses the frozen 15$\times$15 SOM and the frozen 84-dimensional \texttt{fc2} embeddings produced by the LeNet-5 baseline. No CNN retraining or SOM retraining was performed. Training and validation embeddings were analyzed, while the held-out test split remained excluded from development decisions.

The purpose of this stage is to characterize how strongly the learned SOM preserves class structure. Best Matching Unit (BMU) assignments are summarized through neuron occupancy, hit counts, dominant class, cluster purity, normalized class entropy, and class-by-neuron intersections.

\section{Overall Cluster Results}
Both data splits occupied all 225 SOM neurons. The sample-weighted majority-class purity was \textbf{98.94\%} on the training embeddings and \textbf{98.19\%} on validation embeddings. Quantization error was 1.0387 on training and 1.0412 on validation. The close train/validation quantization errors indicate similar prototype-distance behavior across the two development splits, while the small reduction in validation purity reflects modest additional class mixing on unseen in-distribution samples.

\section{Interpretation}
The Week 4 results provide descriptive evidence that the trained SOM contains strong class structure. Every SOM neuron is occupied on both training and validation data, so the 15$\times$15 map is being used throughout rather than leaving large inactive regions. The validation weighted purity of 98.19\% indicates that most samples share neurons predominantly with examples from the same true class.

However, weighted cluster purity is not classifier accuracy and does not imply that every neuron is homogeneous. The purity and entropy maps identify a limited number of mixed regions where multiple digit classes share nearby prototypes. These local regions are especially important because global averages can hide the locations where classification ambiguity is concentrated. The Week 4 cluster analysis therefore provides the spatial basis for Week 5, where classifier errors are mapped onto the same SOM topology.

\section{Leakage Control}
This analysis is development-stage only. The frozen CNN, embeddings, and SOM were consumed read-only. Training and validation embeddings were used to calculate cluster statistics, but the held-out test split was not used to choose cluster-analysis rules, interpret validation regions, or change the model. This preserves the final test split for later evaluation after the analysis procedure is frozen.

\section{Conclusion}
Week 4 converted the qualitative SOM visualizations from Week 3 into quantitative cluster-structure measurements. The 15$\times$15 SOM achieved complete neuron occupancy on both development splits, with validation quantization error of 1.0412 and sample-weighted cluster purity of 98.19\%. The dominant-class map shows broad class-specific territories, while the purity and entropy maps isolate a smaller set of mixed-class neurons that warrant closer inspection.

These results establish the transition from representation visualization to model-diagnostic analysis. Week 5 uses the same frozen SOM and validation assignments to determine whether classifier mistakes are uniformly distributed or concentrated within particular SOM regions and to retrieve representative hard examples from those regions.
